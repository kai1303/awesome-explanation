      Why does 35 appear twice after a split?

      Imagine an index of records numbered by key. You want to add record 35, then retrieve every key from 30 through 42. A B+ tree keeps its entries sorted in small groups called pages. A leaf page holds keys and their records or record pointers. The pages above the leaves hold directions. A page can direct a search to many children, so even a large index can have a short path from its root to a record.

      Choose one child at the root

      The example starts with a root and two leaves. The root holds a separator, 20. Keys below 20 belong on the left. Keys equal to or greater than 20 belong on the right. To insert 35, follow the right pointer. The destination already holds 20, 30, 40, and 50. The leaves sit at the same depth, and each has room for four entries in this example.

      Make room for the fifth entry

      Insert 35 between 30 and 40. Now five entries need four slots. This example splits the page after its second key. The existing leaf keeps 20 and 30. A new right leaf receives 35, 40, and 50. The old leaf’s next pointer leads to the new one, so a reader moving along the leaves sees all five entries in order.

      Copy a boundary into the parent

      How will the next search find the new page? Copy its first key, 35, into the root and attach the new child pointer. The root now divides keys into three ranges. A lookup for exactly 35 follows the rightmost pointer because equality belongs to the right. Its record is still in the leaf. The occurrence of 35 in the root serves as a direction. In a larger tree, an overflowing parent would also split, and a split at the root would add a level. This root has room. Berkeley CS 186 on B+ tree insertion

      Read a range along the leaves

      For the inclusive range 30–42, descend once to the leaf that could contain 30. Skip 20 and collect 30. The page ends, so follow its next pointer. Collect 35 and 40. The next key is 50. Every key after it is at least as large, so the scan can stop. The answer is 30, 35, 40.
